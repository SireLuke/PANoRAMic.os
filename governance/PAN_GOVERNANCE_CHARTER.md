# PAN Governance Charter — Foundational Draft

**Identifier:** PAN-GOV-001  
**Status:** Proposed constitutional baseline; subject to community ratification  
**Scope:** PANoRAMic.os and participating PAN-governed deployments

## 1. Purpose and authority

PAN Governance is the constitutional and institutional coordination layer for PANoRAMic.os. It establishes who may propose, authorize, implement, inspect, challenge, and revise rules.

Governance authorization and technical execution are separate operations. A software component may calculate, recommend, validate, or execute only within an explicitly granted authority. No engine, metric, model, adapter, operator, or institution may grant itself additional authority.

This charter does not claim that PAN currently has a legally constituted public authority or that a software deployment can replace applicable law, democratic institutions, or human rights protections.

## 2. Foundational priorities

PAN's commons-allocation philosophy uses this priority ordering:

1. **Ecological integrity:** prevent irreversible damage and support restoration, conservation, resilience, and regeneration.
2. **Human dignity and essential needs:** protect access to essential services and the unconditional dignity baseline.
3. **Enterprise and productive development:** enable useful production, innovation, trade, and cooperative enterprise within ecological and human constraints.

This ordering guides commons resource allocation. It does not rank the inherent worth or civil rights of people, nor does it authorize discrimination.

## 3. Fundamental principles

- **Dignity:** essential-needs protections must not be conditional on labor, wealth, account activity, or participation in governance.
- **Ecological stewardship:** resource decisions must account for regeneration, scarcity, uncertainty, and long-term impacts.
- **Subsidiarity:** decisions should be made at the lowest level capable of addressing them responsibly.
- **Commons participation:** affected communities should have meaningful access to proposals, deliberation, and review.
- **Transparency with privacy:** publish rules, rationales, public expenditures, and audit outcomes while minimizing exposure of personal data.
- **Due process:** affected parties must have accessible notice, explanation, correction, and appeal mechanisms.
- **Anti-capture:** separate proposal, authorization, execution, and independent verification; disclose conflicts of interest.
- **Reversibility and review:** policies must be versioned, reviewable, and reversible where feasible.
- **Evidence and uncertainty:** distinguish observed data, estimates, model outputs, assumptions, and normative choices.
- **Non-self-authorization:** automated systems cannot expand their own permissions or amend the policies that constrain them.

## 4. Nested governance

PAN should support nested levels, without assuming that every deployment must use the same political or legal structure:

- **Local commons:** community-scale services, stewardship, and operational rules.
- **Regional coordination:** cross-community infrastructure, watersheds, ecosystems, and shared services.
- **Network governance:** interoperability, shared standards, cross-region externalities, and system-wide safeguards.
- **Independent review:** audit, appeals, safety review, and conformance functions that are structurally separated from the operations they inspect.

Higher levels should not displace local authority without a documented cross-boundary reason, a defined scope, and review.

## 5. Participation and representation

The governance framework may use community assemblies, cooperative/worker representation, rotating stewardship roles, and sortition-based review panels.

Sortition and rotation procedures must publish eligibility rules, selection methods, term limits, recusals, replacement procedures, and audit records. Random selection is a representation mechanism, not a substitute for competence, accessibility, or accountability.

The 4-4-1 workforce model and rotary verification may be integrated as operational mechanisms under their own specifications. They do not independently confer constitutional authority.

## 6. Policy lifecycle

Every material policy must have:

1. A proposal with scope, rationale, evidence, expected impacts, and affected parties.
2. Public notice and a reasonable comment period, except for narrowly scoped emergency action.
3. Conflict-of-interest declarations and recusals.
4. An identified authorizing body and a recorded authorization basis.
5. A version identifier, effective date, review date, and rollback procedure.
6. An implementation owner distinct from the authorizing body where practical.
7. Independent verification and an accessible appeal path.

Policy changes must not silently rewrite canonical data, settled transactions, or historical decision records.

## 7. Oversight, disputes, and sanctions

Oversight should include independent audits, public change records, conflict-of-interest review, accessible dispute resolution, and appeal.

Use graduated and proportionate responses where appropriate: correction, education, warning, restricted capability, temporary suspension, and removal for serious or repeated violations. Emergency protective restrictions must be narrowly scoped, time-limited, logged, and subject to prompt independent review.

No sanction may remove unconditional essential-needs protections or extinguish a person's right to challenge the decision.

## 8. Allocation and fiscal boundaries

The ecological → dignity → enterprise ordering guides commons priorities. Specific budgets and formulas require separately versioned authorization.

PAR implements authorized monetary policy and ledger operations. CTA is a native PAN fiscal subsystem that allocates actually collected citizen-tax revenue under authorized policy. RAMS supplies governed resource measurements and operational risk signals; it does not silently mutate PAR balances, settled transactions, conversion rates, or CTA policy.

UBE and the Stewardship/Dignity baseline remain unconditional under the existing PAR policy specifications. Revenue sources and pool accounting remain distinct and auditable.

## 9. Technical authority boundaries

- V8 supplies state-vector representations; it does not determine public policy.
- Gosset 4_21 is a geometric architecture, not a governing authority.
- Janus Hydra translates semantic structures; it does not authorize decisions and is not UHFE or UDFE.
- UHFE and UDFE remain paired formation/dissolution representations.
- NAHT provides history transport and topology capabilities; it does not grant host permissions or decide policy.
- Chronos provides temporal bookkeeping within its declared contract.
- RAMS measures and reports resource conditions within its authorization.
- PAR and CTA perform only authorized fiscal operations.
- RelayWorks implements workforce and cooperative operating rules within approved policy.
- View Ladder and OVT navigate and present information without mutating canonical records.

## 10. Amendment and emergency rules

The final ratification process, quorum, voting thresholds, and protected clauses remain open governance decisions and must not be hard-coded by this draft.

Any adopted amendment process should distinguish ordinary policy changes from charter amendments. Charter amendments require stronger notice, broader participation, recorded rationale, and independent process review.

Emergency authority must be purpose-limited, time-limited, logged, and reviewable. It cannot suspend non-derogable rights, eliminate appeal, or permanently transfer authority to the emergency actor.

## 11. Ratification status

This document is a proposed design baseline. It becomes binding project governance only after an explicit, publicly recorded ratification process. Until then, implementations must label it **proposed**, not **ratified**.
