# NS-EVIDENCE-001 - independent verification (Claude bench)

**Status:** independent numerical verification exercise. This report makes **no claim of global regularity, blow-up, or a solution of the Clay problem.** All runs are smooth, low-mode, periodic, finite-time cases.

Generated by `make_report.py` from `outputs/results.json` and `outputs/comparison_vs_oai.json`; the numbers below are copied from those files by code, not typed.

## 1. Method (derived and implemented from the equations)

Unforced incompressible Navier-Stokes on the periodic box [0, 2pi)^d, nu = 0.01:
`u_t + (u . grad)u = -grad p + nu Lap(u)`, `div u = 0`. Taking the Leray projector `P = I - k k^T/|k|^2` removes the pressure: `d/dt u_hat = -P F[(u . grad)u] - nu |k|^2 u_hat`.

- **Space:** Fourier collocation on N^d nodes; 2/3-rule dealiasing per direction (keep `|k_i| <= floor(N/3)`).
- **Time:** Lawson integrating-factor RK4 (viscous term exact, nonlinear term by RK4 stages).
- **Precision / dependencies:** float64 / complex128; Python 3.13.18, NumPy 2.5.3 (`numpy.fft`), single process, no other dependency. Machine: Linux-6.18.44-fc-v726-x86_64-with-glibc2.39, 2 CPUs.
- **Nonlinear term:** convective form by default. The rotational form `omega x u` is implemented as an independent cross-check (it differs by a gradient that `P` removes).
- **Initial data:** 2D `u = (sin x cos y, -cos x sin y)`; 3D `u = (sin x cos y cos z, -cos x sin y cos z, 0)`.

Diagnostics are volume averages over the box (divide the domain integral by (2pi)^d): `E = 1/2<|u|^2>`, `eps = nu<|grad u|^2>`, `Omega = 1/2<|omega|^2>` (from the curl of `u_hat`), `D = int_0^T eps dt`, `maxdiv = max |div u|` (spectral), balance residual `E(T) - E(0) + D(T)` (ideally 0). `D` is accumulated with the same RK4 stage values as the flow, so it is a fourth-order quadrature; a trapezoid estimate is also stored for comparison with the OpenAI report. Domain-integral values are in `results.json` (`*_integral`).

Analytic `t = 0` values (volume averages): 2D `E=0.25, Omega=0.5, eps=0.01`; 3D `E=0.125, Omega=0.375, eps=0.0075`. Maximum absolute deviation of the solver from them: 2D 0.00e+00, 3D 8.67e-19.

**Result: no disagreement beyond roundoff on any physical quantity** (energy, enstrophy, dissipation rate, integrated dissipation, initial values). Items worth recording, none of them a failure:

1. **Balance residual is a quadrature artifact, as their report says.** Their `-1.3e-11` (3D) and `-5.3e-13` (2D) are reproduced to ~1e-15 by my trapezoid variant (sign convention flipped: theirs is `E(0)-E(T)-trapz`). With RK4-consistent quadrature my residual is ~1e-16..1e-15, and it falls at about fourth order in dt (order test). Their temporal-sensitivity residuals (-9.72e-10, -2.43e-10, -6.08e-11 for dt = 0.004, 0.002, 0.001) fall by a factor of 4 per halving, i.e. they track the trapezoid rule, **not** the solver's temporal error.
2. **Their dt-sensitivity check is below the roundoff floor.** Their E(T) at dt = 0.004/0.002/0.001 differs only at ~1e-14, so it does not test temporal convergence. The order test above, at larger dt, does.
3. **N-scatter in their rows is larger than mine.** Spread across N = 16/24/32 in 3D E(T): theirs 1.10e-14, mine 1.53e-16; in Omega(T): theirs 3.00e-14, mine 4.39e-15. All are roundoff-scale. I did not investigate their source for the cause; a different operation order or normalisation in the FFT bookkeeping would be enough.
4. **2D exact error:** theirs 2.6e-15..1.2e-14, mine 1.0e-15..2.0e-15 at the same dt = 0.002 (both roundoff).
5. **N = 16/24/32 at T = 0.1 is not a convergence study** (their report says as much); the N = 64 reference above shows genuine spatial convergence at T = 0.5. A consequence for their temporal-sensitivity block (3D, N = 16, T = 0.5): their E(T) (0.12127471447955) sits 2.47e-11 above the converged value, a **spatial truncation error** of N = 16, over a thousand times larger than the dt effect they were probing (~1e-14). It does not change their conclusion, but the dominant error in that block is spatial, not temporal.
6. **Divergence near 1e-15 is a consistency check of the projection, not independent evidence of solution quality.**

## 6. Limits of this verification

- My solver and theirs are the same **family** of method (Fourier pseudo-spectral, 2/3 dealiasing, Leray projection, RK4). Agreement shows the implementations are consistent; it does not validate the method against a different discretisation. A second solver of another family (finite volume or lattice) remains outstanding.
- The 2D case does not exercise the nonlinear term. The 3D case does, but only over a short, smooth, low-mode window. No manufactured-solution test with a forcing term, no higher-mode data, no turbulence or near-singular regime.
- Nothing here bears on global regularity, blow-up, or the Clay problem.
